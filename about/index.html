Our story

Studying shouldn't stop when the sun goes down.

For a student without electricity, the school day effectively ends at sunset. Flash Forward exists because that's a problem a few students with a workshop could actually fix.

The problem, made visible

A school day

8 AM–3 PMSchool
3–6 PMDaylight to study (2–3 hours)
after ~6 PMDark
8 AM–3 PMSchool
3–6 PMDaylight
After darkStudy light: up to ~12 hours of light

In much of India and Sub-Saharan Africa the sun sets around 6 PM and school ends around 3 PM — leaving a student only 2–3 hours of daylight to study. 655 million people lived without electricity in 2024 (Tracking SDG7, 2026).

Why education

Why a study light

Flash Forward focuses on education because it has the most compounding long-term effect on a student's opportunities. Light after dark means homework done, revision made, and a fair shot — every night.

For students, by students

For students, by students

High-school and college students in Bellevue build the lights themselves, for students who don't have light to study by. The people solving the problem are the same age as the people living it.

How we work

Three principles

Simple

A $5.17 light that anyone in the workshop can build — no exotic parts, no over-engineering.

Through partners

Local organizations like Brighten Haiti, Loksadhana and Malaika choose recipients — they know their students.

Transparent

You choose where your light goes — one of the partner countries or wherever it's needed most.
