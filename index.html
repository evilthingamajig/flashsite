Together, we can bring light to study by

Student volunteers build $5.17 solar study lights and send them through partner organizations to students who have no electricity after dark.

Why light

Studying shouldn't stop at sunset

Without electricity, the school day effectively ends when the sun goes down. A small solar light gives a student a few more hours to read, revise and finish homework.

Photo: Malaika

655 million

people lived without electricity in 2024. For students, homework ends when the sun goes down.

Where the lights go

Every light goes to a partner that already works with schools and families. The partner chooses which students receive them.

The team

Founded by students

Flash Forward began in 2024 as a student project in Bellevue. The IRS recognized the foundation as a 501(c)(3) public charity in 2025, and student volunteers still run it.
