Tracking Our Work

What our delivery records show, what we are learning, and what remains to be measured.

Share

Our aim is to help students study after sunset with solar-powered light. To explain our progress clearly, we separate what we have delivered from what we hope the light makes possible. Delivery records, partner updates, and personal stories each tell us something different; none should be presented as proof of a change in grades, health, or income.

What We Record

Our public partner delivery record is dated so readers can understand the scope of the figures:

72 lights delivered through 3 partner organizations — as of October 2026

The record includes 42 lights delivered through Brighten Haiti, 15 through Loksadhana in India, and 15 through Malaika in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. You can explore those updates in our Stories hub. These are counts of lights delivered through partners. They are not a count of verified improvements in learning or a claim that every light represents one household.

Four Questions Behind Our Reporting

We want our reporting to help answer four practical questions:

  1. Record.What was built and delivered, through which partner, and when?
  2. Listen.What do students, Builders, and distribution partners tell us about the light and the process?
  3. Improve.Which questions or problems need follow-up from our team?
  4. Coordinate.What information helps the next build and delivery happen more clearly?

1) Record — make the delivery count understandable

A useful count needs context. Our published partner totals identify the organization and the number of lights delivered. We date the totals and keep personal hand-deliveries separate from partner shipments so the same light is not counted twice.

You can start with our impact overview. The distinctions below help explain the limits of these records:

  • Lights, not assumptions:The unit in our delivery record is a light. We do not automatically turn that count into numbers of households, students with higher grades, or years of use.
  • A date and a scope:The 72-light total refers to recorded deliveries through three partner organizations as of October 2026. New activity should be reported with its own date and context.
  • Partner context:Brighten Haiti, Loksadhana, and Malaika work in different communities. Their updates help explain where a delivery fits without implying that the settings or students are the same.
  • Claims need evidence:We have not published a controlled study establishing changes in grades, study hours, health, household income, or emissions. Those outcomes should not be inferred from a shipment count.

2) Listen — understand how the light fits into daily life

Delivery information answers where a light went. Feedback and stories can add context about how it is used and what people want us to understand. These accounts are valuable in their own right and need to be described accurately.

An example: keeping stories in context
Student essays from Dumas, Haiti, describe the role of solar power at Brighten Haiti’s school. They offer students’ perspectives on school electricity. They are not testimonials about Flash Forward study lights.

That distinction matters. A story should identify the program it describes, and a photo or quotation should only be attributed to the person and activity it actually documents. Story submissions go to our team for review before publication.

3) Improve — turn questions into useful changes

Questions from assembly and distribution can help our team decide what to investigate. A build instruction that is unclear, a component question, or a difficulty using the light goes to Engineering for follow-up.

The study light: a practical engineering project

Our design combines a solar panel, rechargeable battery, LED, and printed housing. Engineering owns technical changes and build guidance. Builders can raise questions from assembly, while distribution partners can share questions from use. Each light charges in about 7 hours of sunlight, gives up to 12 hours of light, and lasts one to two years.

The current component estimate is about $5.17 per light, excluding shipping and other costs.

Affordability is one part of the design. Assembly, packing, delivery, and communication also matter. We aim to explain those steps clearly so supporters understand the work behind a light and the limits of a parts-only estimate.

4) Coordinate — connect the people doing the work

Students, Builders, and distribution partners contribute different knowledge. Builders assemble lights. Distribution partners help identify students and coordinate local handoffs. Corporate sponsors can support agreed needs, and our four internship tracks contribute engineering, operations, fundraising, and communication. Keeping these roles clear helps groups find the right way to get involved.

Our public updates should distinguish completed activity from plans. A partnership inquiry is not a confirmed shipment, an assembly plan is not a delivery, and a personal story is not a program-wide result. Questions about a published figure or story can be sent to about@flashforwardfoundation.org.
