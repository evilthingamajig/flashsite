How to put volunteer work on a resume (and internships too)

Put volunteer work and unpaid internships in your Experience section if they involved real responsibilities, formatted just like a job: role, organization, dates and two to four specific bullets. Use a separate Volunteer section only if you have plenty of paid work already.

Want real experience now? Flash Forward's remote internship is open to high school and college students.

By the Flash Forward Foundation team · Updated October 12, 2026

Volunteer work is real experience

If you're wondering how to put volunteer work on a resume, start with this: it counts. Employers, colleges and internship programs care about what you did and what you learned, not whether you were paid. Tutoring, running social media for a nonprofit or coordinating a food drive all show skills that transfer to any role.

The same goes for unpaid internships. An unpaid internship is still an internship, and you should list it as one.

Where it goes on your resume

You have two good options, and the right one depends on how much else you have.

  • In your Experience section. Best for most students. If your volunteer roles or internships involved real tasks, list them alongside any jobs, in reverse chronological order (most recent first).
  • In a separate Volunteer Experience section. Useful if you already have several paid jobs or internships and want to keep the categories clear, or if your volunteer work is mostly one-time events.

If you're a student with little paid work, putting volunteer roles under "Experience" makes your resume stronger and more honest about where you've actually built skills. One-day events, like a single beach cleanup, can be grouped in a short line under Activities instead.

How to format each entry

Format volunteer roles exactly like jobs. Each entry needs:

  1. Your role or title (use the one the organization gave you, or a plain description like "Volunteer Tutor").
  2. The organization's name and location, or "Remote."
  3. Start and end dates by month and year, or "Present."
  4. Two to four bullets starting with action verbs.

Here's a finished entry:

Marketing Intern (Unpaid), Riverside Community Food Bank, Remote, Sept 2025 to Present

  • Planned and scheduled 12 Instagram posts per month for a fall food drive campaign.
  • Wrote captions and edited short videos highlighting volunteer stories.
  • Tracked weekly engagement in a shared spreadsheet and reported results to the communications lead.

You don't need to add "(Unpaid)" or "Volunteer" to the title. Use the title that matches the work and let the bullets do the talking.

Before-and-after bullet examples

Weak bullets describe a role. Strong bullets describe actions and results. Here are common rewrites:

  • Before: "Volunteered at the library." After: "Led weekly reading sessions for groups of 6 to 10 elementary students during the school year."
  • Before: "Helped with social media." After: "Created and posted 3 graphics per week for a nonprofit's Instagram."
  • Before: "Did research for a nonprofit." After: "Researched and added 40 potential partner organizations to a shared database, including contact details and fit notes."
  • Before: "Fundraising volunteer." After: "Drafted sections of a grant proposal, including the project description and budget narrative, for review by the development lead."
  • Before: "Tutor." After: "Tutored two ninth graders in algebra weekly for one semester; both completed the course."

Notice the pattern: action verb, specific task, scale or result. Good verbs include organized, wrote, designed, coordinated, researched, built, led, edited, tracked and presented.

How to list remote roles

Remote internships and online volunteering are increasingly common and are listed the same way. Write "Remote" where the location would go. If the organization is based somewhere specific, you can write "Remote (based in Seattle, WA)."

Remote work shows something extra: that you can manage your time, communicate in writing and finish work without someone watching. You can point to that directly in a bullet, such as "Met weekly deadlines while coordinating with a distributed team over video calls and shared documents."

Online volunteering, like transcribing documents for the Smithsonian Transcription Center or classifying images on Zooniverse, can go on your resume too. List it under Volunteer Experience or Activities with a specific, honest bullet. For more options, see online volunteer opportunities for students.

How to quantify honestly

Numbers make bullets more concrete, but only if they're true. Good things to count:

  • How many: posts, articles, students, events, records, items collected.
  • How often: weekly, monthly, for one semester.
  • How much: dollars raised, people reached, hours of programming delivered.
  • Change: growth from one number to another, if you measured both.

Rules to follow:

  • Don't claim a team result as yours alone. Say "Contributed to" or "As part of a team of five" when that's accurate.
  • Don't guess or round up. If you're not sure of a number, leave it out or use a range you can defend.
  • Don't invent percentages. "Increased engagement" with no measurement behind it is weaker than a plain description.
  • Keep your work log so you can back up any number if someone asks.

If you don't have numbers, describe scope instead: who it was for, how long it lasted and what you were responsible for.

Should you list volunteer hours?

Skip them on most resumes. Hours matter for school service requirements and scholarship forms. If you include them, put them in a bullet or after the dates, like "approx. 3 hours per week." Make sure any hours you report match what your organization signed off.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding real volunteer work at the bottom under a vague heading.
  • Listing duties ("responsible for...") instead of actions.
  • Using the same bullet for every role.
  • Exaggerating titles or results.
  • Leaving off remote or online work because it "doesn't count." It does.

FAQ

Questions

Can I put an unpaid internship under work experience?

Yes. An unpaid internship is real experience and belongs in your Experience section, formatted like any other role.

Should I write "volunteer" in the job title?

No need. Use the title the organization gave you, or a clear description of your role, and let the bullets show what you did.

How far back should volunteer work go?

For high school and college students, include anything from high school onward that's relevant or substantial. Drop older or one-time activities as you gain more experience.

What if I volunteered only a few times?

Group short or one-time volunteering in a single line under Activities. Save full entries for ongoing roles.
