How a Light Is Built

• Our workshop is in Bellevue, Washington, and is run by students.
• Each light is printed, assembled and checked before it is packed for a partner.
• Parts cost $5.17 per light. Volunteers build for free.
• Partner organizations that know the students and communities hand out the lights.

Print the Case

The 3D-printed case holds the light’s components together. The printer lays down the material for each enclosure, and student volunteers run the prints.
The case is the first of six parts. It costs $1.10 in material per light.

Assemble the Light

Volunteers install and connect the solar panel, the LiPo battery, the TP4056 charge board, the two LEDs and the slide switch inside the printed case. They use hand tools and a driver to fasten the panel to the case.

Test the Light

Every light is checked before it leaves the workshop. It must charge in daylight and turn on with the switch.

Pack and Ship to a Partner

Finished lights are wrapped and packed for a partner organization. All 72 lights built so far have been delivered: 42 to Brighten Haiti, 15 to Loksadhana in India and 15 to Malaika in the DR Congo.
Shipping is not part of the $5.17 parts cost.
