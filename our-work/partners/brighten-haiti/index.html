About Brighten Haiti

About Brighten Haiti

Brighten Haiti was founded in 2020 by Kevin Keene to answer a stark fact: fewer than 40% of Haitians have access to electricity, and in rural areas that figure drops below 15%. The organization brings solar power to the schools, hospitals, civic buildings and families that the grid has never reached.

The model is built for scale. Brighten Haiti collects used and discounted solar equipment from the United States, screens and repurposes it, and trains Haitian apprentices to install and maintain the systems. That keeps installation costs near $0.80 per watt, roughly 80% below a comparable U.S. system, and the work earned pv magazine’s 2022 Sustainability Award. Graduates of the apprenticeship have gone on to start solar businesses of their own.

Its motto is a Haitian proverb: “Men anpil, chay pa lou” — many hands make the load light. Flash Forward’s 42 study lights extend that work past the school gate: Brighten Haiti’s school relationships place each light with a student who can keep studying after dark at home.

Their work

Programs at Brighten Haiti

Photo: Brighten Haiti

Solar4Schools

The largest solar-for-schools effort in Haiti’s history, working toward more than 300 schools and nearly 150,000 students. Each school gets a 3kW solar system powering lights, fans and a computer lab loaded with 6,000 books, Wikipedia, Khan Academy and coding courses in Creole, French, English and Spanish.

Photo: Brighten Haiti

Solar Apprentice Program

Haitian trainees learn to install and maintain solar systems, along with the business fundamentals to launch companies of their own — building a local solar workforce instead of flying one in.

Photo: Brighten Haiti

Solar ReUSE

The United States discards more than 13,000 tons of solar panels a year, most bound for landfill. Brighten Haiti screens used panels for safe, productive units and gives them a second life electrifying schools, homes and hospitals in Haiti.

The partnership

How the lights reach students

Flash Forward provides the lights to Brighten Haiti free of charge. Brighten Haiti chooses which students receive each light; the partner knows the students and households. Student interns in Bellevue build the lights; the partner puts them to work.

Tamisade, NS2

The Importance of the Solar System at My School

Before my school had a solar system, there were many difficulties that affected the proper functioning of the classes. Students were not comfortable at all and often lost concentration. Devices like projectors, computers, and printers couldn’t be used, which limited how teachers could deliver lessons and prepare materials.

It caused a lot of problems. The school often had to spend a lot of money on fuel for the generator, but even that didn’t always guarantee lighting. Students would lose time and sometimes couldn’t finish their exercises.

The solar system has brought the school to a modern level. Students can learn technology better, and teachers can prepare their lessons on computers, with better concentration in class. It also brings security because the school stays lit even when it’s dark. In addition, the school reduces its electricity expenses and contributes to protecting the environment.

It’s a big advantage for our community in Dumas because children learn in good conditions, which motivates them to become smarter, more disciplined, and more committed. The solar system is not just light, it’s our hope for the future in Dumas.

What would you like to be in the future?

When I finish school, I would like to become a great doctor so I can help sick people get care and contribute to improving the health of the community. That has been my dream since I was little because I want to serve society with all my heart.

I would like to sincerely thank Brighten Haiti for their support, their commitment, and the love they have for the Dumas community. Thank you for everything you are doing to help us grow, achieve our dreams, and bring more hope for tomorrow. We truly appreciate you!
