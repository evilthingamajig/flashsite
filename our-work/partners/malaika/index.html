Our Work with Malaika

In Kalebuka, near Lubumbashi, Malaika has spent years building an education program around a simple idea: a student’s ability to learn is shaped by much more than the hours she spends in a classroom. The organization began in 2007 and now connects a free school for girls with clean water, health, sport, agriculture, vocational training, and a community center.

Malaika’s school opened in 2011. Today, its website says the school serves 430 girls in primary and secondary education and maintains a 99.3 percent retention rate. The community center extends learning beyond the school day through literacy classes, internet access, sports, and vocational programs for young people and adults.

About the Partnership

Learning does not stop at the school gate

That wider view of education is what makes Malaika a thoughtful partner. Our solar study lights are designed for one practical purpose: giving a student a dependable light when electric service is unavailable. They do not replace teachers, books, or community-led programs. They make those resources easier to use after sunset.

Malaika knows the students, families, and daily routines around its programs. That experience can help determine whether a light is useful, who should receive one, and how to follow up without assuming that every household faces the same circumstances.

What Flash Forward can contribute

Flash Forward is a volunteer-run nonprofit that designs and builds rechargeable solar lights. The work brings young people into engineering, fundraising, media, and operations while creating a practical tool for another student. The lights are now part of education work led in Kalebuka.

Malaika’s local knowledge guides the lights toward students who can use the added hours after sunset.
