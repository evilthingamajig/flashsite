The Study Light

Our Work

A small, rechargeable solar light, built by student volunteers, so that a student without a wall outlet can study after dark.

Purpose Built

A solar panel charges a battery during the day. Two LEDs give reading light after sunset. The parts sit inside a case printed by student volunteers. By our estimate, a light is used about 6 hours a day.

What’s Inside?

Six parts, $5.17 in total. Volunteers print and assemble each light without pay, and the price counts parts only. Shipping is separate.

  • 3D-printed case, $1.10. Printed by student volunteers. Holds every part together.
  • 5V solar panel, $1.50. Charges the battery in daylight.
  • LiPo battery, $1.65. Stores the day’s charge for the night.
  • TP4056 charge board, $0.45. Charges the battery safely from the panel.
  • Two 5 mm LEDs, $0.12. Turn the stored charge into reading light.
  • Slide switch, $0.35. On after sunset, off by morning.
  • Total, $5.17. Parts only.

The ~500 mAh battery recharges in about 7 hours of sunlight and gives up to about 12 hours of light on a full charge. The light is built to last 1-2 years, and replacements are available to partners.

In The Field

Brighten Haiti: 42 lights delivered

Brighten Haiti brings solar power to schools, hospitals and families across Haiti through Solar4Schools. 42 lights delivered to this partner.

Loksadhana: 15 lights delivered

A charitable trust in Maharashtra, India, working on rural education, women’s empowerment and health. 15 lights delivered to this partner.

Malaika: 15 lights delivered

Malaika empowers girls and communities in the Democratic Republic of the Congo through education, health and clean water. 15 lights delivered to this partner.
